Designing a DR strategy that actually meets your RPO

Most DR designs fail not during disasters but during the design phase — when RPO targets are set without understanding what replication technology can realistically deliver.

The number that gets written into DR requirements is usually determined by a business stakeholder who has never deployed a replication solution. "We need zero data loss" is a common starting point. It's also the start of a project that will either blow its budget or underdeliver.

Zero RPO is theoretically achievable — synchronous replication between sites will get you there. But it comes with latency constraints, bandwidth requirements, and infrastructure costs that most organisations aren't prepared for when they write it into their RFP.

RPO vs what technology can deliver

The gap between desired RPO and achievable RPO usually comes down to four factors: inter-site latency, available bandwidth, change rate of the protected data, and the replication technology being used.

  • Synchronous replication: Every write is confirmed at both sites before the application gets an acknowledgement. True near-zero RPO. Requires sub-10ms round-trip latency between sites — practical only for metro distances.
  • Asynchronous replication: Writes are acknowledged locally and replicated in the background. RPO measured in minutes. Works across any distance. Used by Nutanix's built-in replication, Zerto, and VMware Site Recovery Manager in most deployments.
  • Backup-based recovery: RPO measured in hours — the gap since the last backup completed successfully. Appropriate for non-critical workloads where a business can tolerate some data loss.

Where Zerto and Veeam differ

Both are mature, widely deployed platforms. The key difference is at the architectural level: Zerto operates at the hypervisor layer with journal-based replication (seconds-level RPO, continuous replication), while Veeam operates primarily at the backup layer with snapshot-based replication (minutes to hours RPO depending on schedule).

For tier-1 workloads where the business has agreed that 15-minute RPO is the absolute minimum, Zerto is typically the right answer. For tier-2 and tier-3 workloads where hourly or daily recovery points are acceptable, Veeam's replication feature is sufficient and avoids the per-VM licensing cost of a separate continuous replication platform.

The right DR architecture is a tiered one — not every workload needs the same recovery objective, and designing as if they do wastes significant budget.

Testing is the part everyone skips

A DR design that has never been tested is not a DR strategy — it's a hope. Scheduled failover testing, at least quarterly for tier-1 workloads, is what separates organisations that recover from disasters from those that discover their strategy didn't work during the disaster itself.

Zerto's non-disruptive failover testing and Veeam's SureBackup/SureReplica features exist specifically to make this feasible without requiring a production outage. Build the testing schedule into the design from day one — not as an afterthought.
